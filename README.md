# Self-Evolving Physical Deep Learning (SEA) in Nanoelectronics

# Motivation
Emerging deep-learning hardware accelerators based on physical substrates show promise in addressing energy and speed issues of large models. Unfortunately, their computing capacity and flexibility are currently limited by finite computing units and extensive peripheral circuits. Simply enlarging the scale of existing accelerators is insufficient to break the performance bottleneck.

In contrast, the human brain performs high-capacity and flexible computing through evolved neurons that exhibit diversity and heterogeneity, demonstrating remarkable memory and ultra-low processing power—capabilities absent in current accelerators and their algorithms. For example, biological memory is always reconstructed rather than merely stored in specific neurons.

We introduce a self-evolving physical deep learning (SEA) architecture, which incorporates dynamic nonlinearities of  reconfigurable nanoelectronics.

# Basic Example
 Here we provide a prototype example of SEA, which embeds different dataflows in situ into the nonlinear channels of nanoelectronic devices for flexible output distribution conversion. 

# Outlook and Next step
SEA can be used to design various specialized nanoscale physical networks, including demonstrating complex multitasking learning, content generation, and more. These hardware networks have high energy efficiency and flexibility, reducing the number of parameters by 2-3 orders of magnitude, making it easy to deploy and replace advanced models at the edge. SEA is anticipate to stimulate the collaboration of nanoelectronic device communities, algorithm communities, and integrated electronic communities, explore new high-capacity and flexible hardware computing paradigms in the AI era, or inspire improvements and upgrades to existing architectures. Further by designing more powerful nanoelectronic devices in large-scale reconfigurable arrays, highly integrated SEA chips can be developed for complex and advanced applications.
